## One decision I am referring to you rather than making

**G4.3-b has no numeric target.** Challenge 1 established that the brief's G4.3
measures the population where structural merge can hardly go wrong, while
excluding the population where it actually can — the conflicts it auto-resolves,
which G4.4 rewards us for increasing. I added the second measurement because
leaving it unmeasured would be indefensible.

I did **not** give it a threshold, because a target I invented would be my
judgment substituted for yours on the project's central safety property. My
recommendation: **make G4.3-b the primary safety gate and retain G4.3-a as a
regression check.** Until you decide, G4.4's resolution-vs-mis-merge curve
carries the judgment and both numbers appear in every scorecard.

## Early warning on Challenge 9, ahead of schedule

Challenge 9 bet that a hybrid store meets G1.9(b)'s ≤ 1.5× bar against `git gc
--aggressive` on source text, and pre-registered that a measured value above
**1.6×** should be read as evidence that the all-content-through-CDC constraint
itself deserves re-examination.

The ADR-2 prototype has now measured naive content-defined chunking on 400
commits of django (146,344 unique blobs, 2,924 MiB of raw content):

| Store | Size | vs `git gc --aggressive` |
|---|---:|---:|
| `git gc --aggressive` | 108.5 MiB | 1.00× |
| best CDC parameters, per-chunk zstd | 307.9 MiB | **2.84×** |
| whole-file zstd (no chunking) | 788.2 MiB | 7.26× |

**2.84× is well past my own falsification threshold**, and I am reporting it now
rather than at waiver time. Two qualifications, neither of which I want read as
softening it:

- This measures *naive* CDC. The strategy the bet actually named —
  delta-compressing chunks against similar chunks within a pack, to recover
  git's cross-file delta advantage — is **not yet implemented**, and is being
  measured now. The bet is not yet lost; it is behind.
- Git's 27× compression of this corpus comes from delta chains across history,
  which is a genuinely excellent piece of engineering and a harder baseline than
  the brief's framing of git's storage as a weakness suggests. That framing looks
  right for *large binaries* and wrong for *source text with deep history*.

If the pack-level strategy does not close the gap, G1.9 enters §0.5 plateau
review, and the §0.5 cap (no perf waiver worse than 2× target, i.e. 3.0×) is
close enough to the current value that redesign, not waiver, is the likely
outcome. You will see the number either way.
